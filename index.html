Sales

Lead sheets, deals, and pipeline in one flow, from first call to signed contract. Every deal carries its history forward.

Estimating

Estimates that become the budget the job is measured against. Price sheets, quote engine, variance when reality diverges.

Projects

Work in progress, change orders, milestones, and time — tied back to the estimate and the invoices, so "how's this job doing?" has an answer.

Accounting

Invoices, payments, bills, and collections synced from your accounting system, not re-keyed. Aging, concentration, and cash views your books can't give you.

People

Labor costed at the rate actually in effect when the hours were worked, plus utilization and capacity — so margins reflect what payroll really cost.

Intelligence Layer

Intelligence that reasons across your business, not inside one app.

Every tool with an "AI" badge reasons inside its own walls — your accounting system about invoices, your project tool about tasks. Neither can tell you that one customer's slow payments plus one job's labor overrun add up to a cash squeeze in week seven. The Intelligence Layer can, because it holds all five modules at once, and every number it uses has exactly one owner in the platform — so the forecast, the margin screen, and the answer Nexus gives you are the same number, not three versions of it.

Cash-flow forecast

Week by week, thirteen weeks out and beyond, built from your actual receivables, payables, and payroll — not a trend line drawn through last year. Booked versus expected, so you see the squeeze before it lands and know which part of it is already certain.

Margin analysis

Per job and across the book, with labor costed at the rate in effect when the hours were worked. Trend, not just a point in time — so you see margin drifting while there's still time to price the next job differently.

Anomaly detection

Payment timing, vendor behavior, and job-cost patterns measured against your own history, not an industry average. A customer who suddenly pays slower, a vendor whose invoices cluster oddly — surfaced with the evidence, not buried in a report.

Nexus

The assistant built on top of all of it. Ask "what are the biggest risks to cash this month?" and Nexus answers from the same computations behind every screen and shows its work. It cannot invent a figure, because it reads the platform's numbers rather than guessing from raw data — and it is read-only by design: it can tell you, it can't change anything.
